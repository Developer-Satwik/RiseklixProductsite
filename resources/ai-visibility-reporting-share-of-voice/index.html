Direct answer

What KPIs should AEO and GEO reporting track?

AEO and GEO reporting should track AI citation coverage, citation share, brand mentions, answer accuracy, cited URLs, competitor presence, prompt intent, topic clusters, Search Console impressions, click quality, and conversions. The most useful reports separate visibility from trust: a brand can be mentioned often and still be described poorly.

Citation coverage

The percentage of tested prompts where the brand or its URLs are cited. This is the clearest signal that AI systems are using your site as a source.

Citation share

The percentage of citations attributed to your site out of all citations surfaced across the same prompt set. Bing now uses a similar concept inside AI Visibility Insights.

Answer accuracy

The percentage of tested answers that describe the brand, offer, pricing model, audience, service area, and proof correctly.

Commercial prompt coverage

The share of high-intent prompts where the brand appears with a relevant next action, not just generic informational visibility.

Source diversity

The number of unique URLs, internal pages, third-party profiles, reviews, and proof assets being used to describe the brand.

Conversion movement

The downstream change in qualified calls, forms, newsletter signups, demo requests, and assisted pipeline from pages that improved visibility.

Original KPI formulas

A practical AI visibility scorecard.

The Riseklix scorecard turns messy AI answer behavior into a weekly or monthly operating view. It does not pretend to be a search-engine ranking factor. It is a decision tool for prioritizing pages, prompts, and proof.

  1. 01
    AI Citation Coverage = cited prompts / eligible prompts. Use this to see how often your brand or site becomes a source when the buyer question is relevant.
  2. 02
    Citation Share = your citations / all citations on the same prompt set. This compares your source presence against competitors and publishers that AI systems cite instead.
  3. 03
    Answer Accuracy = correct brand facts / tested brand facts. Track whether AI systems get your audience, offer, proof, location, pricing model, and next step right.
  4. 04
    Commercial Relevance = high-intent prompts with a useful CTA / high-intent prompts tested. Informational mentions matter less than visibility where a buyer is choosing what to do next.
  5. 05
    Source Diversity = unique cited URLs / total citations. If all visibility depends on one page, the brand is fragile. Strong brands have service pages, case studies, resource pages, profiles, and third-party proof reinforcing the same facts.
  6. 06
    Content Action Rate = prompts with a clear page fix / total prompts reviewed. A report that cannot produce actions is just observation. Every insight should map to a URL, a content gap, or a proof asset.

Prompt math

How many observations does a useful report need?

A small prompt set can become a serious dataset when it is repeated across surfaces and time. The goal is not huge volume; the goal is a consistent panel of commercially important questions.

  1. 01
    A lean starter report can use 40 prompts across 5 AI/search surfaces over 4 weekly checks. That creates 800 answer observations per month.
  2. 02
    Add 5 named competitors and the same setup produces 4,000 brand-presence cells per month: 40 prompts x 5 surfaces x 4 weeks x 5 competitors.
  3. 03
    Weight prompts by intent. A pricing or agency-comparison prompt should count more than a broad definition prompt because it sits closer to revenue.
  4. 04
    Keep a separate watchlist for branded confusion. Queries such as misspellings, founder questions, social profile searches, and competitor lookalikes can explain why impressions do not become clicks.
  5. 05
    Review source freshness monthly. AI answers often prefer pages with clear dates, updated facts, stable canonicals, and explicit evidence blocks.

Quality control

Why citation reporting needs human review.

A 2026 academic study of Google AI Overviews found that answer generation at scale can cite pages that are not first-page organic results and can include unsupported claims. For brands, the lesson is practical: do not only chase visibility. Audit whether the answers are accurate, sourced, current, and commercially useful.

Not every mention is a win

If the answer describes the wrong audience, outdated offer, old pricing, or incorrect location, more visibility can create more confusion.

Not every cited source helps you

A third-party article, review, social profile, or outdated directory can become the source of truth if your own pages are thin or ambiguous.

Not every prompt deserves action

Some prompts are informational or too broad. Prioritize prompts that indicate comparison, budget, implementation, audit, local fit, or service selection.

Query to action

How to turn AI visibility reports into page updates.

The report is only useful when it creates a shipping queue. Every prompt cluster should tell the team whether to rewrite, consolidate, expand, prove, localize, or leave the page alone.

Cadence

Weekly, monthly, and quarterly reporting rhythm.

AI visibility moves unevenly. A practical cadence keeps teams from overreacting to a single answer while still catching important changes quickly.

Weekly

Check priority prompt changes, new citations, broken crawler access, newly inaccurate branded answers, and pages shipped during the week.

Monthly

Report citation coverage, citation share, answer accuracy, source diversity, Search Console movement, competitor movement, and the next content backlog.

Quarterly

Rebuild the prompt set, refresh benchmark data, retire weak pages, consolidate duplicates, revisit market assumptions, and update executive narratives.
