Direct answer

What are typical pricing models for GEO and AEO agencies?

Most serious AEO and GEO work fits one of four pricing models: a fixed diagnostic audit, a short implementation sprint, a monthly visibility retainer, or an enterprise program that includes monitoring, content, PR, analytics, and governance. The right model depends on how many markets, services, product lines, prompts, competitors, and AI search surfaces the brand needs to monitor.

Audit package

A focused audit is best when the brand has impressions but few clicks or citations. It should produce a scorecard, Search Console query clusters, prompt tests, technical findings, priority pages, and a 30-day fix list.

Implementation sprint

A sprint is best when the audit is already clear. The agency rewrites or expands high-intent pages, adds schema, creates comparison sections, strengthens evidence, and routes visitors to the right offer.

Monthly retainer

A retainer is best when AI visibility is a live channel. It should include monthly prompt tracking, citation review, competitor comparison, content refreshes, proof building, and conversion reporting.

Enterprise program

Enterprise pricing is usually custom because it can include multiple brands, regions, legal review, analytics integration, workflow approvals, PR, ecommerce feeds, and executive reporting.

White-label agency layer

For agencies serving clients, pricing often needs dashboards, client-ready reports, audit templates, prompt libraries, and repeatable delivery playbooks.

Tool-only monitoring

Tool subscriptions are useful, but they are instrumentation. They do not replace strategy, content upgrades, technical fixes, source authority, or offer clarity.

Original framework

The Riseklix 4-layer AEO/GEO budget model.

The cleanest way to budget AEO and GEO is to separate measurement from improvement. A tool tells you where you stand. The work that changes the result is a mix of technical access, answer-ready content, evidence, authority, and conversion routing.

  1. 01
    Layer 1: Diagnostic audit. Review crawlability, indexation, structured data, canonical URLs, Search Console queries, AI prompt presence, cited sources, and competitor visibility. For most small to mid-market brands, this is a 10 to 25 hour project.
  2. 02
    Layer 2: Content and entity sprint. Upgrade the pages already closest to demand: homepage, services, category pages, pricing pages, comparison pages, case studies, and resource hubs. A serious sprint is often 40 to 90 hours because it includes research, writing, schema, internal links, and QA.
  3. 03
    Layer 3: Monitoring rhythm. Track prompt coverage, cited URLs, answer accuracy, citation share, competitor mentions, branded sentiment, and Search Console movement. A lean monthly rhythm is 5 to 20 hours depending on prompt count and reporting depth.
  4. 04
    Layer 4: Authority and proof building. Publish original frameworks, case evidence, founder perspectives, statistics, industry pages, and third-party references. This can be 10 to 40 hours per month when the goal is category-level visibility, not one-off rankings.
  5. 05
    Budget rule. Do not spend 80% of the budget on monitoring. Monitoring without page upgrades gives you a dashboard for a problem you have not fixed.

Buying guidance

Questions to ask before buying an AI visibility audit or AEO/GEO package.

The buyer should test whether the provider understands search fundamentals, AI answer behavior, data limits, and commercial routing. Any provider promising guaranteed citations should be treated carefully because official search guidance still emphasizes quality, crawlability, eligibility, and user value over shortcuts.

  1. 01
    Which prompts will be tracked, and why do those prompts represent buyer demand?
  2. 02
    Which AI search surfaces are included: Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Bing summaries, or another surface?
  3. 03
    Will reporting show cited URLs, competitor citations, answer accuracy, sentiment, and commercial-intent coverage?
  4. 04
    How will Search Console generative AI data and standard search data be used together?
  5. 05
    Which pages will be upgraded first, and what specific sections will be added?
  6. 06
    Will the work include schema, internal links, canonical checks, and crawler access review?
  7. 07
    How often will the team refresh source-heavy pages as numbers, pricing, and platform guidance change?
  8. 08
    How will the package measure pipeline quality, not just impressions or mentions?

Riseklix conclusion

Do not price AEO and GEO by word count.

Word count is a weak pricing unit for AI visibility because the work is not merely writing. The durable value is reducing answer ambiguity. A page earns more visibility when it gives search and AI systems the facts, comparisons, evidence, and next steps needed to explain the brand correctly.

Cheap but weak

A low-cost article package can add pages, but it often misses original data, service context, structured evidence, buyer questions, and internal links. That creates crawlable clutter.

Expensive but useful

A strong package upgrades the pages that already sit near demand, turns scattered claims into answer-ready evidence, and gives the team a way to monitor movement over time.

Best commercial unit

Price around decision-risk removed: clearer service choice, stronger trust, fewer unanswered buyer objections, more accurate AI descriptions, and cleaner conversion routes.

Red flags

What not to buy when someone sells AEO or GEO.

The fastest way to waste money is to buy a shiny label without a measurement method. AEO and GEO work should produce pages, prompts, evidence, and reporting you can inspect.

Guaranteed citations

No agency can guarantee that a third-party AI system will cite a page on demand. The honest promise is better eligibility, clarity, evidence, and monitoring.

Thin FAQ bundles

FAQ sections are useful only when the answers are specific, sourced, and tied to the page. Bulk generic FAQs are easy to publish and easy to ignore.

Tool screenshots only

A report with screenshots but no page-level action map does not improve visibility. The deliverable should say what to fix, where, and why.
