Google AI Overviews: how to check whether your firm is cited.

AI Overviews are the AI-written summaries at the top of many Google results. They answer the buyer before any site is clicked, and they link a small set of sources. Here is how to check whether you are one of them.

By Satwik KumarUpdated 15 November 20262 min read
Time needed

You need
A private browser window
Best for
Service firms and their marketers

The short version

  • Search the questions your buyers ask, not your company name.
  • Note whether an Overview appears, which firms it names and which pages it links.
  • Repeat on another day. Overviews change, and not every search shows one.
  • Impressions without clicks in Search Console often mean you were shown as a source inside an AI answer.

What an AI Overview is, and why it matters

For many searches Google now writes a short answer above the normal results. For "who should I hire" questions that answer often names providers and links a handful of sources. A buyer can read it, form a shortlist and never scroll to the results below.

It is a different product from the Gemini chat app, and its answers can differ. That is why we record AI Overviews separately in every study, alongside ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity.

How to check, step by step

  1. Write ten buyer questions. Describe the problem, not your name. For example: "Who can help a US company hire a UK payroll director?"
  2. Open a private window. This reduces the effect of your own search history.
  3. Search from your buyer's country. Results differ by location. If your buyers are in the US, check from a US location.
  4. Search each question. Note whether an AI Overview appears at all.
  5. Open the sources. Expand the Overview and list every page it links.
  6. Repeat on another day. One check is a snapshot. Two or three show what holds.

What to record each time

  • The exact question, the date and the location you searched from.
  • Whether an Overview appeared.
  • Every firm named in the Overview, in order.
  • Every source linked, and what type it is: company site, directory, list, article or forum.
  • Whether your own website was linked, and which page.
  • Anything wrong or out of date in how you are described.

Why you may see impressions but no clicks

Search Console can show impressions for long, question-style searches with almost no clicks. That often means your page was shown as a source link inside an AI answer. People read the answer and rarely click through.

That isn't wasted. Being a source shapes what the answer says about you. It does mean the page needs a reason to click: a template, a checklist, real data or a clear next step that the summary can't replace.

What tends to get a service firm included

  • Pages that answer the buyer's question directly. Who it's for, how it works, how long it takes and what it costs.
  • A presence where answers look. One study of 3,118 AI answers to "who should I hire" questions, across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, found 50.2% of cited links went to company websites, 25.9% to directories and review sites, and 11.8% to "best of" lists (Continuum, September 2026). That study did not cover AI Overviews specifically.
  • Consistent facts everywhere. The same description of what you do on your site, your profiles and your listings.

We added Google AI Overviews to every Riseklix study in November 2026. Our published figures so far cover ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, so we don't yet have our own numbers for Overviews.

Questions people ask

Does every Google search show an AI Overview?

No. Whether one appears depends on the question, the country and the day. Record the searches where none appears too, because that tells you where the normal results still decide.

Is an AI Overview the same as Gemini?

No. AI Overviews appear inside Google Search. Gemini is a separate chat app. They can name different firms and use different sources for the same question.

Should I check with my company name in the search?

Check both, but treat them differently. A search with your name shows what Google believes about you. A search that only describes the buyer's problem shows whether you make the shortlist.
