Businesses in ChatGPT: what the dashboard shows, and what it misses.

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on 10 November 2026. You can claim your business profile, correct your details and see how often ChatGPT mentions you. It's worth claiming. It also leaves out most of the picture.

By Satwik KumarUpdated 15 November 20262 min read

The short version

  • Claim your profile. It is one more accurate source about you.
  • The dashboard shows mentions, website clicks and discovery topics for the past 28 days.
  • For an online business, a mention only counts when ChatGPT references your website.
  • It doesn't show competitors, the questions asked or any other AI tool.
  • OpenAI says paying doesn't change how often ChatGPT recommends a business.

What you can do with it

The owner, or someone authorised to represent the business, can claim the profile and then update details. A separate identity check is needed before you can publish changes or see insights. The admin can also give an agency access to manage the profile (OpenAI help centre).

Eligible US e-commerce and online-service businesses can also add a Business Agent. Availability depends on location and business type (OpenAI announcement).

What the dashboard counts

MeasureWhat it means
MentionsFor an online business, a mention counts only when ChatGPT references the business's website. The brand name alone doesn't count. For a local business, it counts when ChatGPT shows that location.
Website clicksClicks from ChatGPT to your site.
Discovery topicsThemes in answers that mentioned or cited the business.

Data covers the past 28 days. It is aggregated and only shown above privacy thresholds, so a small firm may see little at first.

Four things it can't show you

1. Recommendations that don't cite your website

In our traced data, ChatGPT showed the firm's own website in 36% of its recommendations (22 of 61). The rest leaned on other sources or showed none. If the dashboard only counts answers that reference your site, a service firm could miss a large share of the times it was actually named.

2. Any other AI tool

Buyers also use Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, and those tools behave differently. In the same data, Perplexity used the firm's own site in 88% of recommendations and Gemini in 11%.

3. Who was named instead of you

A mention count doesn't tell you which competitor got the recommendation, or which pages earned them the place.

4. The questions themselves

You see topics, not the buyer questions behind them. You can't tell whether you appear for the work you most want to win.

What to do next

  1. Claim the profile and correct your description, services and links.
  2. Write the description in buyer language. Say who you help and with what, plainly.
  3. Note what ChatGPT says about you today. That gives you a "before" to compare against.
  4. Measure the rest separately. Record what all five AI tools say when your buyers ask for help, and who they name.

Questions people ask

Does claiming a profile make ChatGPT recommend me more?

OpenAI's help centre says claiming a profile doesn't change your visibility in ChatGPT, and OpenAI has said that paying doesn't change how often ChatGPT recommends a business. The profile helps accuracy. It isn't a ranking lever.

Is Businesses in ChatGPT available everywhere?

It is in beta, and availability depends on your location and business type. Business Agents are limited to eligible US e-commerce and online-service businesses.

Do I still need a wider study if I have the dashboard?

The dashboard covers one tool and counts website citations. It doesn't show competitors, the questions asked, or the other AI tools your buyers use.
