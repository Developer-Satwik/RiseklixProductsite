AI visibility for commercial contractors: what a 50-answer study found.

Developers, general contractors and property managers now ask AI tools for a contractor shortlist. We recorded 50 answers about commercial roofing and siding in one US city, across five AI tools. The contractors that got named had pages that made their work easy to check.

The short version

  • Recognition can sit under the wrong name. One contractor was recommended in 15 of 50 answers, always under its parent company's name and never under its commercial brand.
  • AI shortlists by job. 14 of those 15 came from siding questions. On a flat-roof question the same contractor was named 0 times in 10.
  • Check indexing before content. All nine pages sampled on the contractor's new site were set to stay out of Google.
  • Named contractors publish detail you can check. Scope, place, people, and how they work on a building that stays open.
  • Lists move, patterns hold. Whether the contractor appeared was the same in both passes for 24 of 25 question and tool pairs.

What buyers ask

We studied five buying decisions, each worded the way a buyer would type it and with no company named.

  1. An out-of-town developer choosing a commercial siding contractor for a multifamily project.
  2. The manager of an occupied apartment community replacing siding without disrupting residents.
  3. A general contractor choosing a siding and exterior partner for a mixed-use project.
  4. The manager of a light-industrial building with a leaking flat roof.
  5. A portfolio manager deciding where to spend on roofs and siding.

Each question went to ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, twice, about a day apart.

What AI did with them

Buying decisionAnswers that recommended the contractor
Developer, commercial siding6 of 10
Occupied apartments, siding replacement4 of 10
General contractor, mixed-use exterior4 of 10
Light-industrial flat roof0 of 10
Portfolio roof and siding assessment1 of 10

The tools disagreed with each other too. Google AI Overviews recommended the contractor in 7 of 10 answers and ChatGPT in 6 of 10. Claude and Perplexity didn't recommend it at all.

What the recommended contractors had published

We read the pages behind the firms that kept appearing. These are things AI could describe. They aren't proof of why AI chose them.

  • A page for one kind of job. One firm had a page for new-construction commercial siding that named the building types, the materials and what it fabricates on site.
  • A named local project. Another showed a wall-system project in the same city, with its scope and scale.
  • People and roles. A staff page made it clear who handles commercial work.
  • How they decide. The roof specialists explained how they assess a roof and choose between repair and replacement.
  • How they work on a live site. One explained how it phases work around a building that stays open.
  • A dated example. One firm had an article tying roof assessments to reports and budgets.

Good proof can still be missed

One firm had a detailed case study of reroofing an occupied high-rise, and appeared in only 3 of 50 answers. So an AI shortlist isn't a complete map of who is good. It's a map of whose evidence the tools found for that question.

The check that comes first

When we looked at the contractor's new commercial website, all nine pages we sampled carried a noindex tag. Google says a page marked noindex is dropped from Search, and that a page must be indexed to appear as a supporting link in its AI features.

A setting like this is easy to leave on after a site launch. Check it before you spend anything on content.

What commercial contractors should publish

  • One page for each kind of job and buyer: new-build for developers, replacement on occupied property, preconstruction for general contractors, roof repair for operating facilities.
  • A project page beside each service: the place, the building type, your exact scope and the date.
  • How you work on an occupied site: notices, access, staging, weather protection and daily clean-up.
  • What an assessment delivers, with an anonymised extract of a real report.
  • Who the commercial contact is, and how a buyer starts qualification.
  • How your commercial brand relates to any parent or sister company, in the same words everywhere.

What to leave out

  • Savings, certifications or response times you can't show.
  • Dozens of near-identical pages for nearby towns.
  • Any schema or AI file sold as a guaranteed shortcut. Google says its AI features need no special markup.

Questions people ask

Do developers and property managers really ask AI for contractors?

Buyers increasingly ask AI tools for a shortlist before they contact anyone. We can't tell you what share of your own buyers do this. A study shows what those buyers would be told.

How do I check whether my pages can be indexed?

Open the page, view its source and search for the word noindex, or use URL Inspection in Google Search Console. If the tag is there by mistake, ask whoever maintains the site to remove it.

Would another contractor get the same numbers?

No. These figures belong to one contractor's questions, city and competitors. The pattern, strong for documented work and weak for undocumented work, is what tends to repeat.

How is the firm kept anonymous?

We don't name the contractor, its competitors or its city. The figures are reported as counts of answers out of the total asked.
