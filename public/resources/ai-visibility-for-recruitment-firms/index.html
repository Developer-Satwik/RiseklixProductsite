AI visibility for recruitment firms: what one study of 352 answers found.

Employers now ask AI assistants which recruiter to use before they call anyone. We recorded 352 answers for one UK specialist recruitment firm across 11 hiring situations. The firm was a clear favourite for some hires and almost invisible for others.

By Satwik KumarUpdated 15 November 20262 min read
Study
352 answers, 11 hiring situations
Tools
ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity
Client
Anonymised

On this page

  2. What we found

The short version

  • The same firm was recommended in 27 of 32 answers for an everyday specialist hire.
  • For a confidential leadership replacement: 13 of 32.
  • For a US employer hiring a US director: 4 of 32.
  • The searches with the biggest fees were the ones AI understood least.

What we tested

We wrote the questions an employer would ask for each hiring situation, in four phrasings, and asked ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, running every question twice. That gave 32 answers per situation. The study was recorded before we added Google AI Overviews, so it covers four tools.

What we found

Hiring situationAnswers that recommended the firm
A small UK team hiring a senior specialist27 of 32
A confidential leadership replacement13 of 32
A US employer hiring a US director4 of 32

Same firm. Same website. Three very different answers.

Why the gap exists

For everyday roles, AI had plenty to match: job adverts, team biographies and a clear service page. For leadership search, the proof wasn't public. There was little on what kinds of mandates the firm takes, how confidentiality is handled, or what happened after a placement. So AI hedged, and named someone else.

We see this in most studies. Firms are well documented for their most ordinary work, and vague about the work they most want to win.

What recruitment firms should publish

  • A page for each specialism and seniority level you want to win, not one general services page.
  • The kinds of mandates you take, described without naming clients.
  • How a search runs, step by step, with typical timelines.
  • How confidentiality is handled for sensitive replacements.
  • How fees work, even as a structure or a range.
  • The markets you recruit in, and for employers based where.
  • What happened after placements, such as retention or promotions.

Fix what others say about you

AI also reads directory profiles, old interviews and industry lists. Check that they describe the firm you are now, in the same words as your website. An out-of-date profile can outvote a current homepage.

Questions people ask

Do employers really ask AI for recruiter recommendations?

Buyers increasingly ask AI assistants for a shortlist before they contact anyone. We can't tell you what share of your own buyers do this. A study shows what those buyers would be told.

Would another recruitment firm get the same numbers?

No. These figures belong to one firm's questions, market and competitors. The pattern, strong for documented work and weak for undocumented work, is what tends to repeat.

How is the firm kept anonymous?

We don't name the client, its competitors or the exact roles. The figures are reported as counts of answers out of the total asked.
