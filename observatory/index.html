Observatory rules

A useful number keeps its context attached.

01

Collection date is not decoration.

AI systems, product pages and public evidence change. A September observation should not be repeated as a timeless market fact.

02

Samples keep their boundaries.

A pilot panel, selected public pages and publisher roundups are different populations. We do not merge them into a universal “AI visibility” percentage.

03

Inconclusive stays inconclusive.

Limited retrieval or missing evidence triggers further inspection; it does not automatically become a failing score or an accusation.

04

Commercial interest is disclosed.

Riseklix sells Commercial Discovery and related services. That interest belongs next to the interpretation, not hidden behind it.

Next instrument

From snapshots to a recurring recommendation watch.

The long-term Observatory should rerun a frozen subset of buyer decisions on a declared cadence, preserving question wording, surfaces, timestamps, provider failures, recommendation state, cited sources, and market changes. Until repeated collections exist, we will label the current studies as snapshots rather than pretend they are trends.

Freeze the panel

Keep a declared set of buyer decisions stable enough to compare across collection windows.

Publish the misses

Provider failures and unusable captures remain visible and outside brand-absence denominators.

Track persistence

Report whether recommendation and source movement persists rather than celebrating one changed answer.

Version the method

When the panel, surface, classification rule or collection method changes, the Observatory should say so.
